01 — the mark

The squiggle & the wordmark

one monogram — the double-S squiggle — plus a caps wordmark. together or apart, always with room to breathe.

the squiggle — espresso, on light backgrounds

the squiggle — tan or cream, on dark

Suede Studio

lockup — squiggle + caps wordmark (nav, footer, covers)

  • clear space: leave at least half the squiggle's height empty around every side.
  • the wordmark is always caps, tracked 0.18em: SUEDE STUDIO. big headings elsewhere are Title Case — the wordmark is the exception.
  • colours: espresso or black on light; tan, cream or ice on dark. one colour at a time.
  • the squiggle alone works as favicon, profile picture, and — a signature move — embossed oversized background art at 5–15% opacity on any block.
  • never: stretch it, rotate it upright, add a drop shadow, or place it over a busy photo.

03 — typography

Caps speak. Lowercase explains.

one family, two behaviours: headings are chunky, bold and ALL CAPS — always; body copy is lowercase, regular and smaller. the contrast between the two is the typographic style.

The working font

the deck's display face is a chunky geometric sans. on the web we use Poppins (free, google fonts, weights 400–800) — the closest open match to that rounded, confident feel. if we later license the exact deck typeface, it slots in first in the font stack and everything follows.

04 — tone of voice

Warm. Plain-spoken. Quietly expert.

we write like a knowledgeable friend, not an agency. lowercase in captions and body copy is part of the voice — relaxed, never sloppy.

Warm

“your shop deserves better product photos — here’s how to take them on your phone.”

“businesses must optimise visual assets to maximise conversion.”

Plain-spoken

“your checkout is asking people for too much. cut two fields, sell more.”

“we leverage synergistic funnel optimisation strategies.”

Quietly expert

“we manage ecommerce for a living. this is what actually works.”

“🚀🚀 THE #1 SECRET SHOPIFY HACK THEY DON’T WANT YOU TO KNOW 🚀🚀”

  • captions and body copy: lowercase, first person plural (“we”), short sentences.
  • headings: chunky ALL CAPS. buttons and labels: small tracked caps. body: lowercase. confident, no exclamation marks needed.
  • the punch line device: short caps statements ending in full stops — “the store. handled.” “the busywork. automated.” — used in tickers, tiles and story covers.
  • emoji: sparing — one, sometimes, where it earns its place. never strings of them.
  • never promise “growth hacks”, “secrets” or overnight results. we sell done-properly.
05 — do / don’t

The fast rules

Chunky caps heading black heading, lowercase body, loads of air.
do big black caps headings on white/cream.
start the questionnaire if it's blue, it's clickable.
do tan buttons & accents — straight from the logo.
Ice on black accents pop on the dark feature bands.
do use black bands for drama — one or two per page.
Ice on cream can you even read this? exactly.
don’t put ice text on cream — it fails contrast.
Brown everywhere big brown blocks are the old look.
don’t flood pages with brown — espresso is a detail, not a wall.
Third colour?? no pinks, no gradients, no “just this once”.
don’t introduce extra colours or gradients.
