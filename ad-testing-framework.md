# Ad Testing Framework (Padel) — Low-Risk Budget Edition

A structured creative-testing methodology tuned to your **€500–1,000/month, low-risk** testing budget. The
job of this phase is **learning, not profit.** A small budget means data arrives slowly, so discipline —
clear kill criteria, no emotional decisions, patience for statistical significance — matters more than usual.

> All thresholds are **starting points to validate** against your own account and margins. Pull the exact
> break-even numbers from `financial-model.xlsx`; they drive every kill/scale decision here.

---

## 0. Your numbers (the rules everything keys off)
From the **Realistic** scenario (validate with your data):
- **First-order break-even ROAS ≈ 1.77** (= 1 / contribution margin). Below this, a *new-customer* sale loses money.
- **Target CAC ≈ €37** at €70 AOV (first-order ROAS ~1.9).
- You can tolerate first-order ROAS slightly **below** break-even **only because repeat purchases** (your Klaviyo
  flows) make the customer profitable later — but during testing, judge winners against break-even, not hope.

> Translate these into the metric you'll actually watch day-to-day: **Cost Per Purchase (CPP/CAC)** and
> **ROAS**. Know your break-even CPP (≈ AOV × contribution margin ≈ €70 × 0.565 ≈ **€39.5**). Spend above that
> to acquire and the first order is underwater.

## 1. Platform & structure
**Primary platform: Meta (Facebook/Instagram)** — best for visual, interest-driven sport products and where
padel's community lives. (TikTok is a strong secondary once you have UGC; keep testing to one platform first
on a small budget.)

**Account structure (simple, broad — let the algorithm find buyers):**
- **Testing campaign** (CBO or ABO) → broad/lookalike/interest audiences → **multiple ad sets or one broad ad set with many creatives.**
- Keep audiences **broad** (Meta's AI is good now; narrow audiences waste a small budget).
- **One variable at a time** when isolating: test **creative first** (it's 80% of performance), then offer, then audience.
- Use the **Meta Ad Library** to study what padel competitors are already running before you create.

---

## 2. Budget allocation (€500–1,000/mo)

| Bucket | Share | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Creative testing** | ~70% | Find a winning ad. Most of your budget, especially months 1–2. |
| **Scaling winners** | ~20% | Push anything beating thresholds (grows as you find winners). |
| **Retargeting** | ~10% | Warm traffic (viewers/cart abandoners) — cheap, high-ROAS, but small pool early. |

**Daily math (the honest constraint):** At €600/mo ≈ **€20/day**. To judge a creative you want roughly
**enough spend to see ~1–3 purchases or a clear no** — about **2–3× your target CPP** before deciding
(≈ €80–120 per creative). That means testing only **~2–4 creatives per week**, not 20. A small budget buys
**fewer, more patient** tests. Don't dilute spend across too many ads at once — you'll get noise, not signal.

---

## 3. The testing process (step by step)
1. **Pick the variable.** Start with creative. Build **3–5 distinct concepts** (different *angles*, not just colour swaps).
2. **Launch** at €10–20/ad-set/day (or one broad CBO with the creatives). Don't touch it for **~3 days / ~€60–100 spend** (learning phase — edits reset learning).
3. **Read upper-funnel signals early** (CTR, CPC, thumb-stop, hook rate) to spot obvious duds *before* you've spent full budget.
4. **Let winners accumulate spend** to a decision threshold (~2–3× target CPP).
5. **Kill losers** per criteria below; **iterate on winners** (new hooks, formats, variations of the winning angle).
6. **Document every test** (see log template). Patterns across tests are worth more than any single result.

---

## 4. Kill criteria (cut losers fast — protect the budget)
Kill an ad/ad-set when, after **sufficient spend** (≈2–3× target CPP, ~€80–120):

| Signal | Kill threshold (validate) | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **No purchases** after ~2–3× CPP spend (~€80–120) | Kill | Clear economic no |
| **CPP well above break-even** (e.g. > ~€55–60 vs ~€39 BE) with no improvement | Kill | Losing too much per sale |
| **ROAS < ~1.2** after meaningful spend | Kill | Far below break-even, no repeat will save it |
| **CTR (link) < ~0.8–1.0%** | Kill creative | Hook/creative isn't landing |
| **High CTR but no add-to-cart/purchase** | Fix **page/offer**, not the ad | Traffic is fine; conversion leaks downstream |
| **High ATC but no checkout** | Fix checkout/shipping/price | Friction at the finish line |

**Rules of discipline:**
- **Don't kill on day 1** (learning phase noise) unless spend is clearly wasted with zero upper-funnel signal.
- **Don't edit winning ads** (resets learning) — duplicate to iterate.
- **One change at a time** so you know what moved the result.
- Emotion is the enemy: the data decides, not how much you love the creative.

---

## 5. Scaling rules (when something works)
A "winner" = consistently at/below break-even CPP (or modestly above, justified by your repeat economics) over
**several days and enough purchases** to trust it.

**Scale carefully (a small budget can't absorb volatility):**
- **Vertical:** increase budget **~20–30% every 2–3 days** while ROAS/CPP holds. Bigger jumps reset learning and spike CPP.
- **Horizontal:** duplicate the winner into **new audiences/lookalikes** (e.g. 1–3% LAL of purchasers once you have data) and **new placements**.
- **Creative iteration:** produce variations of the winning *angle* (the angle is the asset; refresh creatives before fatigue).
- **Watch frequency/fatigue:** rising frequency + rising CPP = creative fatigue → refresh.
- **Reinvest, don't overreach:** scale from results, keep a cash reserve; ad spend ties up working capital *before* profit (see business-plan §6).

## 6. Metrics to track (and what they tell you)

| Metric | What it diagnoses | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| **CTR (link)** | Hook/creative strength | < ~1% = weak creative |
| **CPC** | Cost efficiency of clicks | Rising = audience/creative fatigue |
| **Thumb-stop / hook rate (3s)** | Scroll-stopping power | Low = bad opening 3 seconds |
| **Cost per Add-to-Cart** | Interest → intent | High ATC cost = offer/page issue |
| **CVR (LP→purchase)** | Store/offer conversion | Low w/ good CTR = fix store, not ad |
| **CPP / CAC** | The decision metric | vs **break-even CPP ≈ €39** |
| **ROAS** | Revenue efficiency | vs **break-even ROAS ≈ 1.77** |
| **AOV** | Basket size | Lift via bundles/upsells |
| **Frequency** | Fatigue | >~2–3 in small audience = refresh |
| **MER (blended)** | Whole-account truth | Total revenue / total ad spend — the number that actually matters |

> **Watch MER, not just in-platform ROAS.** Meta over-reports; blended MER (all revenue ÷ all ad spend) plus
> your email revenue is the real picture. Reconcile against `financial-model.xlsx`.

## 7. Padel creative angles to test (idea bank)
Test *angles* (the message), not just visuals. Strong padel hooks:
- **UGC gameplay** — real players, real courts (authentic > polished for sport/social).
- **Problem/solution** — "Still playing with dead balls?" / "Grip slipping mid-match?" → your product.
- **Beginner enabler** — "New to padel? Everything you need to start" → **starter bundle** (your AOV hero).
- **Level-up / improvement** — control vs power, choosing the right racket shape.
- **Community/identity** — belonging to the padel movement; club/ambassador content.
- **Gifting** — padel as a gift for a partner/friend (seasonal AOV spikes).
- **Social proof** — reviews, "X players switched," UGC compilations.
- **Founder/player story** — you're a real player; authenticity lowers CAC.

For each: test multiple **hooks** (first 3 seconds / headline) — the hook is the highest-leverage variable.

## 9. The low-budget mindset (read before you start)
- **Patience over volume:** €20/day means slower data. Resist the urge to make decisions on 1 day or €30.
- **Fewer, sharper tests** beat many shallow ones on a small budget.
- **Months 1–2 are tuition** — budget the loss; you're buying market knowledge, not chasing instant ROAS.
- **Creative is the lever:** ~80% of performance is the ad itself. Spend your energy on angles/hooks/UGC.
- **Retention covers acquisition:** because your Klaviyo flows monetise repeat purchases, a near-break-even
  first order can still be a great customer. But never *assume* the repeat — earn it, then factor it in.
- **Stop-loss:** decide your monthly test ceiling in advance and hold to it. Protect the runway that lets you
  reach the months where retention + scale compound into profit.

*Tie every decision back to the model: a winning ad lowers CAC, which raises net margin, which lowers the
revenue you need to hit €10k net. Test like the budget is precious — because it is.*
