# Niche & Product Validation Checklist

A **repeatable, weighted scoring framework** to rank any niche or product objectively — so decisions are
made on evidence, not excitement. Use it for padel sub-segments and for any future idea.

> All scores are *your* judgement against evidence. Gather real data (Google Trends, ad libraries,
> marketplace bestseller ranks, supplier quotes, competitor stores) **before** scoring. Garbage in,
> garbage out.

---

## 1. The six scoring criteria & weights

Score each criterion **1–5** (rubric below), multiply by its weight, and sum for a **weighted score out of 5.0**.

| # | Criterion | Weight | What it measures |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Demand & growth** | 20% | Is the market big enough and trending up (not a dead fad)? |
| 2 | **Margin potential** | 20% | Can you hit ≥ ~55–60% gross contribution after COGS + fees? |
| 3 | **Shipping feasibility** | 15% | Light, durable, compact, EU-sourceable, low return risk? |
| 4 | **Competition / differentiation room** | 15% | Can a new brand carve space (angle, audience, bundle)? |
| 5 | **Ad-creative potential** | 15% | Is it visual, emotional, demonstrable, scroll-stopping? |
| 6 | **Repeat-purchase / consumable potential** | 15% | Will customers come back without new ad cost? (Your edge) |

**Weighted score = Σ (criterion score × weight).** Interpret:
- **≥ 4.0** — strong candidate, proceed to deeper validation.
- **3.0–3.9** — viable but flawed; fix the weak criterion or find an angle before committing.
- **< 3.0** — pass, or only enter with a very specific differentiated wedge.

## 2. Scoring rubric (what each 1–5 means)

### 1) Demand & growth (20%)
- **5** — Large, clearly growing market (double-digit CAGR), rising search/participation, multi-year trend.
- **4** — Solid demand, steady growth, durable interest.
- **3** — Decent demand but flat, or growth is unproven.
- **2** — Niche/thin demand or early/uncertain.
- **1** — Tiny, declining, or a likely short-lived fad.

### 2) Margin potential (20%)
- **5** — Achievable ≥65% gross contribution; strong private-label/consumable margins.
- **4** — ~55–65% contribution; healthy headroom for ads.
- **3** — ~45–55%; workable but tight; needs high AOV/retention.
- **2** — ~35–45%; survives only at scale or with strong repeat.
- **1** — <35%; structurally hard to profit after CAC.

### 3) Shipping feasibility (15%)
- **5** — Light, small, durable, non-fragile; fast EU supplier; minimal returns.
- **4** — Manageable size/weight; EU options; low-moderate returns.
- **3** — Some bulk/weight/fragility or sizing-driven returns.
- **2** — Heavy/bulky/fragile → high shipping cost or breakage.
- **1** — Oversized/hazmat/electronics-heavy returns nightmare.

### 4) Competition / differentiation room (15%)
- **5** — Underserved; weak/no brands; obvious angle or audience to own.
- **4** — Competitive but clear room via bundle/audience/brand.
- **3** — Crowded; need a sharp wedge to stand out.
- **2** — Saturated; dominated by entrenched brands on the core product.
- **1** — Hyper-saturated commodity, race to the bottom.

### 5) Ad-creative potential (15%)
- **5** — Highly visual, emotional, demonstrable; natural UGC/community; "thumb-stopping."
- **4** — Strong visual/story hooks available.
- **3** — Needs effort to make compelling; generic.
- **2** — Hard to show value visually.
- **1** — Boring/undemonstrable; no creative angle.

### 6) Repeat-purchase / consumable potential (15%)
- **5** — True consumable or frequent replenishment; subscription-friendly.
- **4** — Strong cross-sell/accessory ladder; recurring need.
- **3** — Occasional repeat or upgrade cycle.
- **2** — Mostly one-time; weak ladder.
- **1** — Pure one-and-done purchase.

## 3. Worked examples (scored)

Scores below are illustrative judgements from the 2026 research — **re-verify before acting.**

| Criterion (weight) | 🏆 **Padel** | Rucking / weighted vest (women 40+) | Hyrox-adjacent gear | Aroma diffuser (wellness) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Demand & growth (20%) | 5 | 4 | 5 | 4 |
| Margin potential (20%) | 4 | 4 | 3 | 4 |
| Shipping feasibility (15%) | 4 | 2 | 3 | 3 |
| Competition / room (15%) | 3 | 3 | 3 | 1 |
| Ad-creative (15%) | 5 | 4 | 5 | 3 |
| Repeat / consumable (15%) | 5 | 2 | 3 | 5 |
| **WEIGHTED SCORE** | **4.35** | **3.25** | **3.70** | **3.40** |

**Why padel wins:** top-tier demand/growth + a genuine consumable ladder (balls/grips) + outstanding
creative potential (a visual, social, community sport). Its only soft spot is **competition on the core
racket** — which is exactly why the strategy enters on accessories/consumables/bundles first.

**Why the others scored lower:**
- *Rucking/weighted vest* — heavy product (shipping = 2) and weak repeat (durable, score 2) drag it down,
  despite a great underserved audience (women 40+). Viable, but harder logistics and thinner LTV.
- *Hyrox-adjacent* — explosive demand and creative, but trademark limits and mixed margins/repeat.
- *Aroma diffuser* — strong consumable economics, but **competition = 1 (hyper-saturated)** kills it for a
  small budget. Good economics can't save a market you can't get cheap traffic in.

## 4. Beyond the score — qualitative gut checks

A high score is necessary, not sufficient. Also confirm:

- [ ] **Would I (or my customer) genuinely buy this?** Personal/audience empathy lowers CAC and improves creative.
- [ ] **Is there an existing engaged community?** (Subreddits, clubs, hashtags, events.) Communities = cheap reach.
- [ ] **Can I see competitors *spending* on ads?** (Meta Ad Library.) Active spend = validated demand. No ads at
      all can mean no demand *or* untapped gap — investigate which.
- [ ] **Is there a clear hero + add-on ladder?** (Consumable → accessory → bundle → private-label.)
- [ ] **Can I get an EU supplier or fast fulfilment?** (See `supplier-sourcing.md`.)
- [ ] **Is the AOV mid-ticket (€50–90) or buildable to it via bundles?**
- [ ] **What's the realistic return/sizing risk?**

### 🚩 Instant red flags / disqualifiers
- Patent/trademark/brand-protected core product you'd be infringing.
- Regulated claims (medical/health) you can't legally make.
- Fragile, oversized, hazmat, or battery-shipping-restricted goods.
- Pure one-time purchase with no ladder **and** thin margin.
- Hyper-saturated commodity with no differentiation and entrenched giants.
