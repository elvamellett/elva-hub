Brand directions · August 2026

Two ways to wear the same three colours.

Navy, espresso and the gold already in your logo. What changes between the two directions is not the palette — it is which colour leads. You are looking at Harbour.

Why navy

You are a Brighton and Hove developer. Navy is the argument, not the decoration.

The office is on Ship Street. Kings House stands on Hove Lawns with uninterrupted sea views. Montpelier Place sits in the heart of Brighton and Hove. The next developments are coastal. Navy carries all of that at once — sea, Regency ironwork, and the register a developer is expected to hold — without a word of explanation in the copy.

Espresso keeps it warm. A navy-and-white site drifts corporate very quickly; a warm dark brown in the deep sections and the footer stops that happening, and it gives the gold something to sit against.

The gold does not change. It is sampled directly from your existing logo. That matters commercially as much as visually: this is a new ground for a mark you already own, not a rebrand that throws away what people recognise.

Legibility

Checked, not assumed.

Every pairing below is measured against WCAG AA, which asks for 4.5:1 on body text. This is why the secondary grey is darker than it looks like it wants to be — a paler grey fails, and the failure only shows up once real copy is in.

PairingRatio

One rule
The gold reaches about 2:1 on the light ground. It is an accent, a rule and a hover colour — never body text. On the dark sections it passes, which is where the footer call-to-action lives.

Type

Your logo is a Didone. The headings should be too.

The current site sets headings in a typeface unrelated to the wordmark, so the logo and the headlines never quite speak to each other. Bodoni Moda is the same species as your logo — high contrast, sharp serifs — which resolves that in one line of code.

It is set at 600 and above throughout. The thin-heading problem flagged earlier was a weight problem, not a Didone problem.

All three families are on Google Fonts under the Open Font Licence. Free, self-hostable, no licence fee in any direction. Type licensing is a cost that catches people out — this avoids it entirely.
