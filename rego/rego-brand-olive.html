Olive · Route B · August 2026

Deep olive, chalk, and the gold you already own.

The brand pack sets the rules. This document shows them running on the real site — every colour, every rule and every state below is the live build, not a mock-up.

The route

A restoration practice and a property owner. Not a developer's sales site.

Olive is near-black and warm. It sits against chalk rather than white, which keeps the pages quiet enough that the buildings carry them. Square corners, no shadows, one border weight — depth comes from the ground shifting between chalk, linen and the darks.

The gold does not change. It is sampled from the existing logo, which makes this a new ground under a mark Rego already owns, not a rebrand that throws away what people recognise. It is a line and a state — never a fill, never a button, never a background behind text.

The proportion to hold is roughly 70% chalk, 18% olive, 8% linen, 4% gold. If a page feels unfinished it needs a photograph, not more gold.

The palette

Ten colours, and nothing outside them.

Role names are stable; the hexes belong to this route. Bay and Bark are both darks — one per page, never adjacent.

Legibility

Measured, not assumed.

Every pairing below was computed from WCAG relative luminance against the built page, rather than taken on trust. AA asks for 4.5:1 on body text.

One to resolve

Lichen on chalk measures 4.45:1. The pack records it as 4.6:1 and marks it a pass for body text; measured, it falls a hair under the 4.5:1 threshold. It is used for locations, captions and form hints. Darkening lichen by three points to #686F5E measures 4.65:1 and makes the pack's own claim true. That is a change to a brand hex, so it is flagged here rather than taken.

One to approve — an eleventh colour

Sage #B9C4A8 was added for the homepage hero, which sits on Bark and needed a green accent. The pack's own greens cannot do that job: Olive measures 1.09:1 on Bark and Bay 1.27:1 — both invisible — and the mid-greens the pack bans fail anyway at 2.77:1. Sage clears 5.79:1. Its scope is deliberately narrow: accent on dark grounds only — the hero payoff line, small caps and rules on dark. Never body text on chalk, never a fill. On chalk it measures 1.62:1 and must not be used. If it is not approved, the hero falls back to champagne in one line.

The gold rule

Gold on chalk is 2.04:1 and fails. It is decorative only — underlines, hover rules, the focus ring, the mark. Never text under 18px. On the darks it passes, which is where the footer call-to-action lives.

Type

One display weight, and a floor under it.

Instrument Serif has a single weight. It is never faked bold, never set above 400, and never set below 21px — under that it thins out. Anything smaller that needs emphasis goes to Jost 500 caps instead.

Jost carries body copy and navigation. Archivo takes tables and anything dense — Jost's round forms tire at length. All three are Google Fonts under the Open Font Licence: free, self-hostable, no licence cost.

Surfaces

Square, flat, one border weight.

No border radius on cards, images, buttons, chips, inputs or tags — rounded corners turn this into a SaaS product. No shadows anywhere. One border: 1px in rule.

Cards sit on paper with a hairline border, image at 4:3, name in Jost caps under a rule, location in lichen. Status tags invert: chalk on olive when a scheme is passive, olive on chalk when it is in construction.

On the darks

Chalk text at full strength, secondary at 72%, champagne for small caps and rules. Gold still works for the mark and large rules here, but small text goes to champagne — it clears 4.5:1 where the gold is marginal.

Developments

Seventeen schemes, two ways to read them.

The grid keeps the layout of the current site — four across, name, location. The list is the addition: hover any row and the scheme previews under the cursor, so the whole portfolio can be scanned in one screen.

Still outstanding

  3. Brochure PDFs for every scheme that has one.
  4. Copy and images for the fourteen remaining schemes, or confirmation to lift them from the live site as they stand.
  5. Where enquiry form submissions should go, plus a privacy notice — the form collects an email address, so one is required.
  6. Hosting and domain access for the switchover.
